About

I am an M.S. student in Computer Science at Georgia Tech, studying while employed. I study how large language models acquire, store, and update knowledge reliably, through retrieval and through parameter editing, and how to evaluate these systems so that real gains can be told apart from artifacts of the experimental setup.

My work spans knowledge editing, reliability of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and evaluation methodology. I also work as a researcher at the Yangtze River Delta Digital Finance Industry Research Center, on fintech and digital currency. Before moving into computer science, I studied social policy and data analytics at the University of Pennsylvania and economics (mathematical track) at Fudan University.

Research

Knowledge Editing for Large Language Models
2026 – present · advised by Tianci Liu (University of Tennessee)

Unstructured knowledge editors often reproduce an editing passage but fail to recall its individual facts without that passage. I am a co-first author of FOVEATED, which gives each sentence a focused view by shifting RoPE positions during editing only, and improves five editors on two LLM backbones and three benchmarks. I also reproduced and diagnosed editing baselines such as MEMIT, AlphaEdit, AnyEdit, and UnKE.

Reliability and Evaluation of Retrieval-Augmented Generation
2026 – present · independent research, while employed

When one false claim is copied or paraphrased across many nominal sources, it forges a consensus that agreement-based reliability methods trust. Effective-Source Reliability merges near-duplicate sources before estimating reliability. A related study shows that AI-generated rewrites stay relevant under standard retrieval metrics while hiding whether evidence comes from independent sources.
