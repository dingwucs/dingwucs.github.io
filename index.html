Ding Wu

I am an M.S. student in Computer Science at the Georgia Institute of Technology, studying while employed. I am interested in how large language models acquire, store, and update knowledge, both through retrieval and through parameter editing, and in evaluating these systems carefully enough that real gains can be told apart from artifacts of the experimental setup.

Since 2026 I have been a remote research intern with Tianci Liu at the University of Tennessee, working on knowledge editing. I also work as a researcher at the Yangtze River Delta Digital Finance Industry Research Center. Before moving into computer science, I studied social policy and data analytics at the University of Pennsylvania and economics (mathematical track) at Fudan University.

In a passage being edited, later sentences already have low loss because earlier sentences give them context, so the editor underestimates how much it still has to learn. Editing on RoPE-shifted views of each sentence removes this shortcut and improves recall of individual facts.

When copies of one false claim appear under several nominal sources, agreement-based reliability scoring counts them as independent votes. Merging near-duplicate sources into effective sources before scoring keeps the copies from outvoting the truth.
